Who shops here, and what should we do about each group?

A mall's marketing team has one budget and one newsletter. They want a small number of customer groups that are real (they don't change if we resample), distinct (well separated), and actionable (each one implies a different campaign).

There is no label to score against, so "accuracy" doesn't exist. Instead the pipeline is judged on separation (silhouette), stability under resampling (bootstrap ARI), agreement across algorithms, and whether segments hold up on held-out customers.

Correlations

Income and spending are almost uncorrelated, so they carry independent information. Gender barely relates to anything.

Why standardize?

k-means measures Euclidean distance. Unscaled, a 10-unit change in income (k$) would count the same as 10 spending points, and age (range ~50) would weigh differently from income (range ~120). Every feature set is z-scored inside the pipeline so each feature contributes on the same scale.

Gender is binary. After z-scoring it becomes a ±1 jump that can split every segment in two by gender alone. The tournament tests whether including it helps (it doesn't).
