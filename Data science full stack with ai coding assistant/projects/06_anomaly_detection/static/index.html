Page the on-call engineer before the machine fails, not after.

Every missed failure is downtime; every false page erodes trust until alerts get ignored. The goal: catch as many real failure windows as possible, as early as possible, within a tolerable false-alarm budget (≈ a few per week).

Failure labels are never used to train a detector or pick a threshold, only to grade the result. In production you don't have labels for the next failure, so a benchmark that tunes on them overstates performance.

Five detectors, all causal

Rolling robust z|x − median| / MAD over the trailing day. Fast, explainable, robust to outliers in the window.
Seasonal residual zDeviation from the median of the same time on the past 7 days. Ignores normal daily cycles.
Isolation ForestRandom splits isolate outliers in few steps. Uses 6 window features (level, change, deviation from yesterday, volatility, slope).
Local Outlier FactorIs this point in a sparser region than its 35 nearest "normal" neighbours?
EnsembleMean of each detector's percentile rank, so scales are comparable.
Thresholds: a detector alerts when its score beats the 99.9th percentile of its own probation scores. That's ~1 expected alert per 1,000 normal points, chosen with zero knowledge of the failures.

Honest read: no detector dominates. Isolation Forest catches the most failures, LOF has the cleanest alerts (best PR-AUC and alert precision), and the simple robust z-score is the quietest (0.3 false alarms/week). The ensemble averages away some of each detector's strengths. A stacked or vote-based ensemble would be the next experiment.
